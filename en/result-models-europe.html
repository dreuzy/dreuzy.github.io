Scientific result 03

From catchment to Europe: which model for which question?

The challenge is no longer only to build a good model for one site. It is to make models comparable, reproducible and deployable, and then determine which level of representation is needed for a given catchment, available data and scientific question.

Central idea

Model selection itself becomes part of modelling. There is no universally optimal level of complexity: relevance depends on context and purpose.

HydroModPy: turning a model into a reproducible workflow

HydroModPy organizes catchment delineation, data integration, parameterization, calibration, simulation and spatial diagnostics within a single Python environment. The issue is not only software: the goal is to make explicit and comparable steps that often depend strongly on modeller choices.

The workflow represents groundwater levels, seepage areas, cumulative fluxes, trajectories and residence times and compares several formulations on the same catchments.

Upscaling requires distinguishing properties

Regional work on the Armorican Massif seeks to separate transferable lithological effects from variability caused by weathering, fracturing and local conditions. Rather than assigning one value to a geological unit, this leads to distributions of properties.

At European scale, work presented at EGU 2026 estimated effective transmissivity for 3,333 crystalline headwater catchments. Values span several orders of magnitude and reveal regional organizations. Comparison with hydraulic tests also highlights an important limit: an effective catchment property is not equivalent to a local measurement.

FutureFlow: multiple fidelity levels

FutureFlow formalizes three levels of representation: parsimonious hillslope models, 3D MODFLOW-type models and integrated surface–subsurface models. Each has different computational cost, data requirements and validity domains.

The scientific question becomes: what level of fidelity is needed to reproduce the process of interest? The answer should depend on geology, topography, dominant processes, observation density and simulation objective.

Three scientific shifts

From model to comparison framework

Models are evaluated through common processing chains with comparable data and diagnostics.

From one value to a distribution

Hydraulic properties are described with their variability and at the scale relevant to the model.

From implicit to explicit choice

Fidelity is no longer an arbitrary prerequisite; it becomes something to select and justify.

Scientific contribution

My specific contribution lies at the interface between hydrogeology, scientific computing and upscaling: formalizing robust modelling workflows, comparing representation levels, and relating local properties, effective properties and domains of validity. This work is currently developed with Clément Roques, Alexandre Gauvain, Ronan Abhervé, Hélène Hivert, Alexandre Boisson, Laurent Longuevergne and partners at Inria, the University of Neuchâtel, BRGM and Géosciences Rennes.
