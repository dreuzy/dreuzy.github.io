Research

My research focuses on water circulation and solute transport in subsurface environments, with particular emphasis on shallow aquifers and headwater catchments. I combine observations, environmental tracers and modeling to understand how these systems function and to develop approaches that are robust and parsimonious enough to be deployed beyond intensively monitored sites.

Shallow aquifers and headwater catchments

Near-surface aquifers play a major role in sustaining streamflow and water availability, yet their hydraulic properties often remain poorly constrained. We investigate how geology, geomorphology and groundwater–surface-water interactions organize subsurface flow.

We develop methods to estimate hydraulic properties from data available at broad scales, including discharge, stream-network extent and intermittency, geological information and groundwater-level observations. These approaches are progressively being deployed from hillslope to regional and European scales.

Transit times, water quality and reactivity

The time water spends underground controls much of the transport and fate of contaminants. We use environmental tracers, including CFCs and dissolved silica, together with flow and transport models to characterize groundwater residence-time distributions.

A substantial part of this work concerns nitrate. We investigate where denitrification occurs within aquifers, how it depends on geological structure and transit times, and to what extent these properties can be transferred from one catchment to another.

Water resources in a changing environment

We use this process understanding to investigate how water resources evolve under climate change and human pressures.

Applications include low flows and stream intermittency, future water availability, drinking-water supply systems, and risks associated with groundwater rise in coastal areas. The objective is to connect process-based models with anticipation and water-management questions at the territorial scale.

Modeling and numerical tools

Modeling is the methodological thread running through this research. Following earlier work on flow and transport in fractured media, we now develop models that represent surface–subsurface interactions and can be deployed across large numbers of catchments.

This effort is embodied in HydroModPy, a Python toolbox for building, calibrating and comparing shallow-aquifer models at catchment scale.
