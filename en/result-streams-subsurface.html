Scientific result 01

Reading the subsurface from streams

The properties of a shallow aquifer are difficult to observe directly. Yet part of the information is visible at the surface: stream networks develop where the water table intersects topography. We have used this relationship as a hydrogeological calibration constraint.

Key result

Stream-network extent can become a hydrogeological observable in its own right. Across 24 headwater catchments in north-western France, the method correctly reproduces network extent in about 80% of cases.

Why the question is difficult

Headwater catchments are numerous, small and often poorly instrumented. Hydraulic tests remain local, whereas models require effective properties at catchment scale. By contrast, stream networks and their permanence can be observed over large areas. The challenge is to determine whether this surface information can genuinely constrain subsurface functioning.

The principle

The model simulates water-table position and seepage areas. The simulated network is then compared with the observed one. Calibration seeks to reduce both missing stream segments and simulated segments in excess.

This turns readily available geographical information into information on the effective hydraulic conductivity of the aquifer.

What we learned

1. Low-order streams contain strong information

Calibration is especially sensitive to the extent of small streams, precisely where surface–subsurface interactions are most direct.

2. A mean property is not always enough

When discharge data are added, conductivity and storage capacity can be distinguished. Two catchments with comparable conductivities can behave very differently if their effective porosities differ.

3. Intermittency becomes a calibration observable

At Canut and Nançon, expansion and contraction of the stream network complement discharge and help constrain model parameters with low uncertainty.

From pilot catchments to deployment

The aim is less to provide the local value of a property than to estimate effective properties consistent with model scale. This logic enables deployment across ensembles of catchments and feeds HydroModPy and FutureFlow.

It also requires distinguishing scales: a pumping test and an effective catchment property do not measure the same thing, even when their orders of magnitude can be compared.

Scientific contribution

This line of work is part of a broader approach I have pursued for several years: inferring subsurface properties or structures from information available at larger scales. Here, the specific contribution has been to make the observed stream network — and then its intermittency — a quantitative constraint for calibrating shallow-aquifer models, in close collaboration with Ronan Abhervé, Alexandre Gauvain, Clément Roques, Laurent Longuevergne and Luc Aquilina.
