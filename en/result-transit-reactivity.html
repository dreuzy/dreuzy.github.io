Scientific result 02

From residence times to biogeochemical function

Time spent by water in the subsurface is essential, but it is not sufficient to predict contaminant fate. Our work has progressively shown that we also need to know where flow paths go and where reactive zones are located.

Key result

Two waters of the same age can undergo very different transformations. For nitrate, vertical stratification of reactivity and access to reducing horizons are decisive.

Why residence times?

Environmental tracers connect water observed today to its recharge and circulation history. They provide access not to a single age but to a distribution of transit times, which acts as an intermediary between aquifer structure and transport or transformation processes.

Strongly stratified reactivity

Work with Tamara Kolbe and project collaborators showed that denitrification capacity depends on the position of reactive zones relative to flow paths. The base of the weathered zone or layers rich in reduced minerals may concentrate most of the reactivity.

The key conceptual result is that the total amount of reactivity is not enough: its spatial location within the hydrogeological architecture controls its effectiveness at catchment scale.

Three stages in how the question evolved

1. Measuring transit times

CFCs, dissolved silica and models constrain distributions of groundwater residence times in shallow aquifers.

2. Linking transit times and reactivity

Reaction depends on exposure time, but also on whether water actually encounters electron donors and reactive interfaces.

3. Upscaling

The question becomes whether geology, geomorphology, climate and land use can predict integrated retention or transformation functions across headwater catchments.

Tracers as observers of system dynamics

At Ploemeur, a CFC record spanning 2004–2024 shows that transit times respond very differently to changes in pumping depending on hydraulic connectivity. After pumping was reduced, median transit time increased by about +15 years at the pumping well, +10 years at F11 and +5 years at MF1, while other points changed little.

Residence time thus becomes not only a system descriptor, but also an observer of transient reorganization.

Towards Aqua Incognita – Aqua Cognita

The current extension is to use residence times and redox conditions as intermediate variables between landscape characteristics and biogeochemical functions. The ambition is no longer simply to date water, but to understand how far a hydrosystem stores, delays, transforms or transmits the pressures applied to it.

This combines process-based causal relationships with statistical relationships when the determinants are numerous or less directly observable.

Scientific contribution

My contribution has been to make residence time a link between flow structure and biogeochemical functioning, and then progressively to move from the question of water age to the functional capacity of hydrosystems. This trajectory was developed with Tamara Kolbe, Camille Vautier, Jean Marçais, Sarah Leray, Luc Aquilina, Gilles Pinay, Flore Sergeant and many international collaborators.
